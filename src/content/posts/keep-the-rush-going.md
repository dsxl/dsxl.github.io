---
title: Keep the Rush Going
description: >-
  Costa Rica, a borrowed GoPro helmet, and eight zip-lines down a volcano —
  where a phrase I'd been saying for years finally made sense to someone else.
date: 2013-08-15
---

It was August of 2013 and I had just purchased a new GoPro Hero 3 that I
couldn't wait to use. The next day, I was flying out to Costa Rica on an
adventure tour.

I remember sitting on the flight over to San José and I couldn't sit still.
Adrenaline was flowing through me, a tingling sensation of all the adventures
ahead. It was uncontrollable — my mind was exploding with ideas of where I could
put that camera for the most unique perspectives I could think of.

Upon arriving in Costa Rica, I was the most awkward tourist you could come
across. I had to take pictures of everything and everyone. I was on the streets
squatting and rolling on the floor for the right shot. It was ridiculous, but
I'd do it all over again.

We went fishing in Puerto Jiménez, on the Osa Peninsula. It was a beautiful town
that enjoyed life without technology. Watching the sunset in the west when we
went horseback riding to the top of the mountain, clearing paths with a machete
to get fresh coconuts and sugar cane, watching whales jump out of the water, and
seeing insects the size of Pokémon — those are memories I will always have of
that place.

However, that truly special moment didn't happen until later in the trip.

## Arenal

During the last few days, we went to Arenal Volcano. We didn't have any set
plans; it was just an interesting site we wanted to visit.

The drive there was a story in itself, involving record-breaking rainfall,
massive landslides, and us ending up on Costa Rica's national news when we
needed directions amidst the chaos in a small village.

We toured the volcano site and had no idea it was home to one of the world's
most intense zip-lines. All tours for that day were sold out. But as fate would
have it, one of the instructors who was last to go up offered me his spot and
gear — it even had a GoPro mount on the helmet. It was meant to be.

I was the only one in the gondola riding to the top to meet the rest of the
group, who were patiently waiting for me. Thanks, guys.

After introductions and safety, we set off on eight zip-line routes that would
take us back down to the mountain base. Top speeds of 55 MPH and drops of 550+
feet below you. A thrill of a lifetime.

I befriended two guys who were Miami locals and adrenaline junkies. We instantly
bonded and traded stories about the different adventures we'd been on.

## The fourth route

We had just finished the fourth route — the fastest one, the one that caps out
at 55 MPH. Our hands were shaking from adrenaline and excitement. We were all
coming in hot and crashing into the safety pads.

Without hesitation, my two adrenaline junkies and I hopped over to get on the
next line. We told the guide not to wait for the others. He asked us why. I
looked at one of the guys and, in harmony, we said:

> Keep the rush going.

It was in that moment that it all felt so blissful. I had been saying that
phrase for years to my family and friends and they didn't get it. To share a
moment in harmony with someone else who *did* get it was amazing.

From that point, the idea for **keep the rush going** was born.

To inspire others to adventure out into the unknown. To live outside your
comfort zone. To truly feel alive instead of just being alive.

I hope my story inspires others to get out there and see this beautiful home we
call Earth.
